Privacy

The short version

Meet has no accounts and keeps nothing about you on its server. Your starting point, the people you add and the addresses you keep are saved only on your device.

To find a meeting place and directions, map positions and addresses have to be sent to the map, search and routing services listed below. Nothing is sold, and there are no ads or analytics.

What's saved on your device

  • Your starting point.
  • The people you add: the names you give them, their addresses and positions, and how each one travels.
  • Addresses you keep with the heart, your past meetups, and your settings.

These are kept in your browser's storage for this site. Meet doesn't set cookies or receive a copy, and they don't move between your devices. They stay until you delete them: remove people and forget addresses in the app, use the button below, or clear this site's data in your browser.

Done. Meet has nothing saved on this device.

Your location

Meet uses your location only if you tap the arrow on the start screen and allow it when your browser asks. It's used once, to look up your address, which is then saved on your device as your starting point. Meet doesn't follow your location in the background.

You can change your starting point in the menu at any time, and turn off location access in your browser's or phone's settings.

Other people's addresses

When you add someone, you're giving Meet their address. Please add only people who are happy for you to use it to plan a meetup. Their details are kept on your device, like yours, and are sent to the services below only as map positions and addresses, without names.

When you send a meeting place, it goes only to the app and people you choose, and contains only the place, the time and any note you add.

What's sent to other services, and why

Like any website, each of these services sees your device's IP address. Each gets only what it needs to answer:

Address search: Photon (komoot) and Nominatim (OpenStreetMap)
What you type in an address box, or the position of a spot you tap on the map or of your location, to find its address.
Map: OpenFreeMap, or OpenStreetMap's map servers
The part of the map you're looking at.
Places: Overpass (OpenStreetMap) and Photon
The area around the meeting point, to list places to meet.
Walking, cycling and driving routes: FOSSGIS routing service
The start and end of each route, from your browser and through Meet's server.
Public transport: Transitous
The start and end of each trip and the time, through Meet's server.
Photos: Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons
Which place a photo is wanted for.
Google Maps, when it's switched on
The map, directions and travel times through Meet's server (map positions), and Google's card for a place you open, which your browser loads from Google. Google may set its own cookies. See Google's Privacy Policy.
Tripadvisor, when it's switched on
The names and positions of places, through Meet's server, for ratings, photos and reviews. Nothing about you.
Fonts: Google Fonts
Your browser loads Meet's typeface from Google.

Meet's server

  • It passes requests on to the routing, public transport, Google and Tripadvisor services, so their keys stay secret and the same answer can be reused.
  • It keeps recent answers, which contain map positions but no names, in memory for up to a day, and remembers which map places match a Google or Tripadvisor listing. None of this is linked to you.
  • It uses your IP address to limit how much each visitor can ask at once, and forgets it within ten minutes.
  • Meet's host, Render, may keep short-lived technical logs of requests (such as IP addresses and the addresses requested) to run and protect the service.

Your choices and rights

Everything Meet keeps about you is on your own device, so you can see, change or delete it yourself at any time. Depending on where you live, you may have rights over your personal data, such as to see or delete it. Because Meet's server doesn't keep personal data, there's usually nothing on our side to provide or delete, but you're welcome to ask.
